About Me

I am a Ph.D. student in Human-Centered Computing at Georgia Tech, advised by Thad Starner and Alex Adams.

My research lies at the intersection of affective computing, human factors, and accessibility. I develop unobtrusive sensing and adaptive intervention technologies that support affect regulation and human performance without disrupting ongoing activities. I also explore AI-enabled approaches to making learning more accessible for neurodivergent learners and people with disabilities.

Research interests

Three areas, one human-centered goal.

Designing technologies that recognize human needs and respond with care, clarity, and access.

Affective Computing & Human Performance

Human–AI Interaction

◎

AI for Accessibility

AI-enabled learning tools that reduce barriers for neurodivergent learners and people with disabilities.

Research approach

Evidence into interaction.

My work combines human factors, experimental psychology, user research, and interactive system prototyping. I investigate how physiological, behavioral, and interaction data can inform intelligent technologies that respond to human needs while minimizing disruption to primary tasks.
