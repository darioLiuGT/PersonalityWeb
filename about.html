About

What I Do

I am a Ph.D. student in Human-Centered Computing at Georgia Tech. My research focuses on affective computing and human-centered AI, with an emphasis on developing seamless sensing and intervention technologies that support affect regulation and optimize human performance without disrupting primary tasks.

I am also interested in designing AI-powered accessible technologies that enhance learning experiences and reduce barriers for neurodivergent learners and people with disabilities.

Approach

Drawing on psychology, human-computer interaction, and user experience research, I study how physiological, behavioral, and interaction data can support people without interrupting their primary tasks—and how AI can reduce barriers to learning.

I care about building systems that do more than capture signals. They should respond with clarity, care, and meaningful access.
