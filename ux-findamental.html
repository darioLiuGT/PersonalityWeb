01

Background

Our team worked with Dr. Wayne Phoel, a Visiting Research Engineer at the University of Maryland’s Institute for Systems Research, on digital solutions for financial literacy among international students in the U.S.

We used contextual design principles, qualitative research, and data consolidation to uncover needs and translate them into solution directions.

02

The problem

Graduate international students experience stress while managing local financial situations across transitions between countries, schools, and jobs.

Financial information can be fragmented, unfamiliar, and difficult to trust when students are also adapting to a new educational and social context.

03

Research goals

What financial stressors do international students face when transitioning between countries, schools, and jobs?

We aimed to understand emotions, experiences, technology use, information gaps, desired access, and the sources of financial information students found most trustworthy.

04

Methods

Semi-structured interviewsExplored stress, trust, jobs, financial literacy, and demographics.
Affinity + persona analysisConsolidated qualitative evidence into patterns and representative needs.
Wall walkMade evidence visible and helped the team identify relationships across findings.
Visioning sessionsMoved from insights to opportunity areas and product concepts.

05

Design direction

Reduce uncertaintyMake financial information easier to discover at the moment students need it.
Build trustConnect learners with credible sources they can understand and act on.
Respect transitionsDesign for changing needs across study, employment, and relocation.
Turn insight into actionTranslate research findings into understandable product concepts.
